04

Order From Randomness

The chaos game begins with a point and a collection of vertices. A vertex is selected at random and the current point moves a fixed fraction of the distance towards it. The new position is plotted, another vertex is selected, and the process repeats.

With three vertices and a jump ratio of one half, the apparently random process produces the Sierpiński triangle. Large regions remain permanently empty even after hundreds of thousands of iterations.

Changing the number of vertices, the jump ratio or the rules used to select the next vertex can produce radically different fractal structures from the same basic algorithm.
